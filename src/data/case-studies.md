## 01 · Ecological Network Website Revamp
### Challenge
- Overline: The challenge · Heading: **Make a technical programme feel human.**
- The programme covers a large ecological network, with initiatives, partners, publications and media spread across many pages. The content was dense and written for specialists.
- The goal: help the public understand why the programme matters, then let interested visitors dig deeper without getting lost.
### Process: "From three concepts to one story"
Description: Explore wide, then commit to the direction that tells the story best.
1. **Explore three concepts**: Proposed three distinct concept directions before committing to one, so stakeholders could compare tone and structure side by side.
2. **Structure as a story**: Ordered the journey as a narrative: introduction, why it matters, the ecological network, then strategic partners.
3. **Review with C.L.E.A.R.**: Checked key screens against the C.L.E.A.R. UI framework: copy, layout, emphasis, accessibility and reward.
4. **AI-assisted Hi-Fi**: Drafted Hi-Fi screens with Claude, tied to the design library, then refined every screen by hand.
### Key decisions: "Why it works this way"
- Lead with the story, not the org chart. Visitors meet the "why" before the details.
- Progressive depth for dense content: parallax storytelling, map cards and side panels reveal detail only when visitors ask for it.
### Screens: "Story-driven, built from one library"
Description: Desktop and mobile views from the delivered design.
### Outcome: "What shipped"
- Launched to the public
- Pages delivered: Home, Initiatives, Publications, Media Gallery, FAQ, Contact and error states
- Interactive components: parallax story sections, map cards, initiative side panels

## 02 · Light Rail Line Microsite
### Challenge
- Heading: **Introduce infrastructure as a story about people.**
- A new rail line comes with technical detail: alignment, specifications, progress updates, traffic announcements and public inspection documents.
- The microsite had to make that information easy to find, while presenting the line as something that connects communities, not just a construction project.
### Process: "Narrative first, then structure"
Description: The concept came from the line's own story and design principles.
1. **Concept from narrative**: Shaped the visual direction around the line's narrative and design principles, then proposed it to the client.
2. **Content architecture**: Organised the site into clear sections: the line, technical specifications, project progress, traffic announcements and public inspection.
3. **Responsive delivery**: Desktop, tablet and mobile layouts were delivered so every audience gets a fitted view.
4. **Scannable patterns**: Used bento grids, carousels, timelines and hover states to keep long content light and easy to scan.
### Key decisions
- People-first hero imagery frames the line as community infrastructure before any technical detail appears.
- Bento grids for benefits: each point stands on its own, so visitors can scan instead of read.
### Screens: "One concept, four screen sizes"
Description: Desktop and mobile views from the delivered design.
### Outcome: "What shipped"
- Launched to the public
- Delivered with responsive layouts for desktop, tablet and mobile
- Sections delivered: line overview, technical specifications, project progress, traffic announcements, public inspection, error states

## 03 · State Religious Council e-Services Portal
### Challenge
- Heading: **One portal for every citizen's errand.**
- Citizens needed to pay, check application status and apply for assistance, tasks that used to be spread across separate channels.
- The portal had to put those tasks up front, work for first-time and returning users, and stay clear for an audience of every age and digital skill level.
### Process: "On-site, flow by flow"
Description: Requirements came straight from the people who run the services.
1. **On-site discovery**: Spent 4 days at the client's office gathering requirements directly with stakeholders.
2. **10 wireframe flows**: Mapped and presented 10 wireframe flows to the client for review and sign-off.
3. **Lead the room**: Led client meetings solo and guided a junior team through Hi-Fi design and handoff.
4. **Release by release**: Designed across releases: guest access, login entry points, quick actions, chatbot and feedback forms.
### Key decisions
- Task-first homepage: Pay, Check and Apply quick actions sit above the fold, so the most common errands take one click.
- Built-in accessibility tool and an announcement bar keep key information reachable for everyone.
### Screens: "Services up front, details on demand"
Description: Desktop and mobile views from the delivered design.
### Outcome: "What shipped"
- Launched to the public
- 10 wireframe flows presented and signed off with the client
- Delivered: guest homepage, login entry points, quick actions, chatbot, feedback forms

## 04 · Expressway Operator Website Refresh
### Challenge
- Heading: **Two public websites, one consistent experience.**
- The project covered two expressway websites used by the travelling public. Both needed a refreshed look and layouts that work on every screen size.⏎⏎In the original website, information was hard to find, the hotline was easy to miss, visuals inconsistent, and people browse mostly on mobile.
### Process: "Full involvement, start to finish"
Description: Stakeholders, heuristics and responsive design across the whole project.
1. **Stakeholder management**: Managed stakeholder input across the full project, from kickoff to delivery.
2. **Heuristic review**: Used Jakob Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics to guide and justify design decisions.
3. **Responsive design**: Designed responsive layouts so every page adapts from desktop to mobile.
### Key decisions
- Heuristics as a shared language: grounding feedback in usability principles keeps discussions about users, not taste.
- Responsive from the start: desktop and mobile layouts planned together, not one after the other.
### Screens: "Two sites, one approach"
Description: Views from the delivered design.
### Outcome: "What shipped"
- Launched to the public
- Two expressway websites refreshed
- Responsive layouts for desktop and mobile

## 05 · State Religious Council Website Revamp
### Challenge
- Heading: **Serve the public, and the team that runs the site.**
- An official website has two audiences: the public looking for services and information, and the council staff who update content through the CMS.
- The revamp had to work for both.
- The website also aligns with the state government initiative for digitalisation.
### Process: "From the client's office to launch"
Description: Requirements in person, then design, motion, testing and handover.
1. **On-site requirements**: Gathered requirements in person at the client's office.
2. **Motion graphics**: Created motion graphics for the website.
3. **UAT facilitation**: Facilitated user acceptance testing with the client before launch.
4. **Design documentation**: Wrote design documentation, including a CMS guide for composing hero banner images.
### Key decisions
- Document for the people who maintain it: a CMS composition guide helps staff upload images for banners that keep the design intact.
- One shared component library (navigation, cards, tabs, timelines, accordions) keeps every page consistent.
### Screens: "One library, three screen sizes"
Description: Views from the delivered design.
### Outcome: "What shipped"
- Launched to the public
- UAT facilitated with the client before launch
- Design documentation handed over, including a CMS image guide

- Heading: **Enterprise workflows on a set framework.**

- It also needed a clear answer for use beyond the desktop.
### Process: "One designer, end to end"
Description: Lead the design, stay inside the framework, and plan for mobile.

## 09 · Shariah Commodity Trading Platform
### Challenge
- Heading: **Make complex trading flows easy to follow.**
- Commodity trading involves multi-step transactions with strict rules. Users need to complete them accurately and with confidence.
### Process: "Simplify, systemize, refine"
Description: Fix the flow first, then make every screen consistent.
1. **Simplify flows**: Broke complex trading flows down into clearer steps.
2. **Apply the design system**: Applied the design system across screens for consistency.
3. **Client feedback sessions**: Worked through feedback sessions with the client and refined the design.
### Key decisions
- Simplify before styling: a clearer flow makes every screen easier to use.
- Consistent components lower the risk of mistakes in high-stakes transactions.
### Screens: "Clear steps for complex trades"
Description: Views from the delivered design.
### Outcome: "What I delivered"
- Complex trading flows simplified
- Design system applied across the platform
- Design refined through client feedback sessions
